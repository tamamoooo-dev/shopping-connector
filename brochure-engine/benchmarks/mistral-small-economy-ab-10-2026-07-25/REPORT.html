Mistral Small vs Medium · economic A/B · 25 July 2026

Is Medium worth 11× Small?

Ten crops lifted unchanged out of the frozen 50-crop benchmark, run through the frozen production configuration twice. The only thing that changed between the two runs is the model string. Everything below is measured, not estimated.

Controls: identical frozen bytes (sha256-verified per crop against frozen-sample.json), identical Verbatim prompt read out of the same source file (sha256 e643b2a1…), temperature 0, top_p 1, reasoning_effort: none, json_object, no OCR, one request per crop, identical parser, identical scorer, identical ground truth. The scorer was proved equivalent by re-scoring the full 50-crop Medium run through it and diffing against the published metrics — byte-identical. Small: 10/10 valid JSON, zero retries, zero failures, every response confirmed mistral-small-latest. No production code, model or data was changed.

The sample

Ten crops, ten retailers, ten categories — five that Medium scored clean on the 50-crop run and five where it already had at least one miss, so the set is not stacked toward either outcome. Selection is purposive, not random: it was chosen to span easy and hard products, English captions, prices and package sizes, exactly as briefed. No new images were introduced. Each card notes why that crop is in the set.

What the failures actually are

Where Medium succeeds and Small fails — 7 field cells, 6 crops

Almost all of it is perception, not reasoning. Small misread a large red 17.95 as 11.95 (crop 10), duplicated a letter to produce REEEM from a clearly printed REEM (crop 13), and dropped the superscript St from St Michel (crop 30). None of these are hard crops; Medium read all three correctly. Two further cells are prompt following: Small folded the on-pack Break lockup into the caption (crop 19) and spliced 6×250 ml into the middle of Pepsi Bottles (crop 40). The remaining cell is the serious one — see below.

The one that should decide this

On crop 33 Small returned package_size: "10 KG" for a bag that prints 5kg in the caption and ٥ كيلو in Arabic. Ten does not appear on the image. That is hallucination, not misreading: a well-formed, plausible, confidently-stated pack size that is exactly double the truth, on a staple where price-per-kilo is the entire basis of comparison. Medium got it right. A shopper-facing price engine that doubles a pack size is worse than one that returns nothing. Small also attached the attribute fresh to a pineapple crop that never prints the word (crop 26) — the same tendency, lower stakes.

Where Small succeeds and Medium fails — 1 field cell

Crop 44: Small read the struck-through 45.51 exactly; Medium truncated it to 45.5. A real perception win on a small figure, and worth recording — but one cell against seven. Small also earned an unscored point on crop 8, where it preserved the brochure's printed misprint Jmbo while Medium silently corrected it to Jumbo, which the frozen prompt explicitly forbids. Small lost that crop anyway on caption spacing.

Where both fail identically

Eight cells, and they are all already-known Medium constraints, not new information. Crop 8 reproduces the documented current-price role inversion — both models returned the crossed-out 44.99 as the selling price on a crop whose price is a large 30 marked "now". Crop 30 is a shared silent null on both prices. Crop 26 is the documented per-piece convention. Switching model does not fix any of them, and the deterministic price guard that is already owed would catch crop 8 under either model: two prices are printed, one was returned.

Confidence remains noise

Small reported 0.98 on the fabricated 10 KG, 0.98 on REEEM, and 0.98 on the inverted price — the same 0.98 it reported on its clean crops. Medium's uncalibrated confidence carries over to Small unchanged. Nothing here should ever gate acceptance.

What this sample can and cannot support

Ten crops is enough to expose failure modes and to compare the two models against each other on identical inputs. It is not enough for a precise accuracy rate: at n=10 a single crop moves any field by 10 points, and the sample was deliberately balanced rather than drawn at random, so neither column estimates a 50-crop rate. The 7:1 head-to-head split is not statistically significant on its own (exact binomial p ≈ 0.07). The cost measurement, by contrast, is exact — it is arithmetic on reported token counts, and it will hold at any sample size.

Recommendation — Option A

Keep Medium in production. Do not document Small as an economical mode.

Small is genuinely 11.4× cheaper and about 25–30% faster, with identical JSON reliability — the operational case is real. But it lost 6 of 50 field cells to Medium and won 1, and the losses are concentrated in exactly the fields this product sells on: it fabricated a pack size at double the truth, corrupted a brand token that doubles as the Browse slug, and misread a plainly legible previous price. At $0.00205 per crop, Medium's absolute cost is not the constraint on this system; the saving is roughly $0.0019 per offer.

Option C was considered and rejected on the measured evidence. An “economical mode” is only safe if its failures are quieter than the primary model's, and Small's are louder: hallucinated sizes and corrupted brands are silent, well-formed and confident, so they would enter the corpus indistinguishable from good rows. Documenting Small as a supported mode would mean accepting that class of error somewhere in the pipeline. If cost ever does become the binding constraint, the measured place to revisit this is a Small-first / Medium-escalation split gated on a deterministic validator — which needs the price guard built first, and a larger sample than this one to size the escalation rate.
